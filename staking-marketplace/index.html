Introducing a
Staking Marketplace
to a Multi-Chain Wallet

A bid market for delegated stake, built into a multi-chain crypto wallet. Delegators post what they hold and node operators bid commission terms against it.

Case structure

I designed a marketplace layer inside an existing multi-chain wallet that inverts how staking works. Instead of picking a validator from a list sorted by advertised yield, the holder posts what they want and node operators bid commission terms against it. The goal was to introduce commission, uptime and slashing risk without turning a deliberately simple wallet into a validator research tool.

ClientNDA
Year2025
RoleLead Product Designer
PlatformiOS

This feature introduced a two-sided market into a wallet that was intentionally simple. Before designing interfaces, we needed to define how someone would find it, understand what commission actually costs them, and see slashing exposure before committing rather than after.

What we had to solve

  • Find a natural entry point for Stake Desk inside existing navigation
  • Handle multi-chain and multi-account context without fragmenting the experience
  • Keep delegator flows lightweight, with a request published in a couple of steps
  • Make commission, uptime and slashing risk readable by someone who is not a validator researcher

What we did

We used stakeholder workshops, journey mapping, and quick wireframes to align on product structure early. That gave us a shared foundation for navigation, entry points, and delegator flows before committing to visual design.

Delegators are holders with idle assets, not professionals. The work was about keeping request creation extremely simple, and making competing bids comparable at a glance.

  • Publish a delegation request in minimal steps
  • Identify the strongest bids using badges that surface track record, not just the lowest commission
  • Accept several bids within a single request to split stake across operators in one action
  • A position dashboard focused on clarity, grouped by health so a jailed or slashed delegation surfaces first

Because this was a new feature, we focused on tracking the moments where the experience would either work well or break down.

Request-to-Delegation Rate

Percentage of published requests that become active delegations after bid acceptance and on-chain confirmation. Shows whether users can complete the full flow.

Stake Retention Through First Unbonding

Percentage of delegators who re-delegate rather than withdraw after their first unlock. Shows whether the product built trust, not just a transaction.

Time to First Bid

Time between publishing a request and the first operator bid. Helps measure marketplace liveness and request visibility.
